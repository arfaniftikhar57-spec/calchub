Savings plans and financial goals

This guide explains how to use the calculator, includes an example, discusses limitations and answers common questions.

Savings calculators estimate how regular deposits and assumed interest may build towards a future balance or target.

How to use this calculator

Enter starting savings, contributions, time and any assumed rate. Compare a no-interest scenario with an interest-bearing scenario to see the role of deposits.

Worked example

Saving £100 each month for 12 months adds £1,200 before any interest, assuming all deposits are made.

When this calculator is useful

Useful for emergency-fund planning, holidays, deposits and understanding monthly savings targets.

When not to rely on it

Rates may change, interest can be taxable and inflation reduces future purchasing power. Some accounts restrict deposits or withdrawals.

Practical tip

Set a realistic monthly contribution and review your target when income or costs change.

Frequently asked questions

Is interest guaranteed?

Only according to the terms of a specific savings product; rates may change.

Does the result include inflation?

Usually not. Future balances may buy less than the same amount today.

What if I miss a deposit?

Your final balance will generally be lower than the regular-contribution estimate.
