How percentages work

This guide explains how to use the calculator, includes an example, discusses limitations and answers common questions.

A percentage expresses a part of a whole per hundred. The same numbers can answer different questions depending on the calculation you select.

How to use this calculator

To find p% of x, calculate x × p ÷ 100. To find what percentage a is of b, calculate a ÷ b × 100, provided b is not zero.

Worked example

15% of 240 is 36. An increase from 80 to 100 is 25%, because the change of 20 is divided by the original 80.

When this calculator is useful

Useful for schoolwork, business reporting, tax, shopping discounts and comparing relative changes.

When not to rely on it

Percentage change depends on the starting value; going up 20% and then down 20% does not return to the original amount. Division by zero is undefined.

Practical tip

Write down which number is the base before calculating a percentage.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between percentage and percentage points?

A change from 10% to 15% is 5 percentage points, but a 50% relative increase.

Why is a 20% rise then 20% fall not zero?

The second 20% applies to a different, higher starting amount.

Can percentages exceed 100%?

Yes. For example, 150 is 150% of 100.
