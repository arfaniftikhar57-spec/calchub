Scientific calculator guide

Scientific calculators extend ordinary arithmetic with powers, roots, trigonometric functions and logarithms. Correct input order and angle mode matter.

How to use this calculator

Use parentheses to group operations. Check whether trigonometric functions expect degrees or radians. Distinguish a negative sign from subtraction when entering expressions.

Worked example

The square root of 144 is 12. sin(30°) equals 0.5, whereas sin(30 radians) has a different value.

When this calculator is useful

Useful for engineering, science, mathematics homework and checking intermediate calculations.

When not to rely on it

Results may be rounded due to floating-point arithmetic. Some functions are undefined for particular inputs, such as division by zero or real logarithms of negative numbers.

Practical tip

Estimate the answer first and verify angle settings before trusting trigonometric results.

Frequently asked questions

Why is sin(30) not 0.5?

Your calculator may be using radians rather than degrees.

Why do I see a long decimal?

Many calculations cannot be represented exactly in finite decimal or floating-point form.

What does a domain error mean?

The function is not defined for that input under the calculator’s supported number system.
