How ROI is calculated

Net profit = Final investment value − Initial investment − Additional costs. ROI (%) = Net profit ÷ Total amount spent × 100, where total amount spent includes initial investment and additional costs.

ROI is a simplified estimate and does not account for timing, inflation, tax or risk. Verify figures before making financial decisions.

Return on investment explained

This guide explains how to use the calculator, includes an example, discusses limitations and answers common questions.

ROI compares net gain or loss with the original amount invested. It is a simple percentage measure, not a complete risk or time-adjusted performance metric.

How to use this calculator

ROI % = (ending value − initial investment) ÷ initial investment × 100, assuming relevant costs are already reflected in the values.

Worked example

Investing £200 and receiving £250 back gives £50 profit and 25% ROI before additional costs or tax.

When this calculator is useful

Useful for comparing simple business projects, campaigns and investment outcomes.

When not to rely on it

ROI ignores how long the investment took and may omit fees, cash flows, inflation and risk. It is undefined when initial investment is zero.

Practical tip

Compare projects over similar time periods and include all material costs.

Frequently asked questions

Can ROI be negative?

Yes. If you recover less than you invested, ROI is negative.

Is ROI the same as annual return?

No. ROI alone does not account for the length of the investment.

Should fees be included?

Yes, when assessing real profitability, relevant costs should be included.
