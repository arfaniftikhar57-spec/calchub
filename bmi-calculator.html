How to use this calculator

BMI is weight in kilograms divided by height in metres squared (kg/m²). For example, 70 kg at 1.75 m gives a BMI of about 22.9.

Body mass index explained

This guide explains how to use the calculator, includes an example, discusses limitations and answers common questions.

BMI is a quick screening ratio between adult weight and height. It does not directly measure body fat, fitness or overall health.

How to use this calculator

Use weight in kilograms and height in metres: BMI = weight ÷ height². Enter accurate measurements without shoes where practical.

Worked example

A person weighing 70 kg at 1.75 m has a BMI of about 22.9 (70 ÷ 1.75²).

When this calculator is useful

Useful as one population-level screening indicator and for following a broad trend alongside other health measures.

When not to rely on it

Adult BMI categories are not appropriate for children or teenagers without age- and sex-specific growth charts. BMI may misclassify muscular people and does not account for pregnancy or body composition.

Practical tip

Discuss concerning results with a qualified clinician rather than making decisions from BMI alone.

Frequently asked questions

Is BMI a body-fat percentage?

No. It is a ratio based on height and weight, not a direct fat measurement.

Can athletes have a high BMI?

Yes. Greater muscle mass can increase BMI without indicating excess body fat.

Should I use adult BMI for a child?

No. Children need age- and sex-specific growth references.
